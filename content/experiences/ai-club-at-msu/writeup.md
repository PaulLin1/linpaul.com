I joined the AI Club at Michigan State University as the secretary for the executive board in 2024. At the time, I had little experience in AI and was elected solely based on leadership skills. During the first couple of months I served as a secretary, I redesigned the club's member database pipeline using Zapier and Notion. I engineered a solution for maintaining the information of our 1000+ members, helping reduce membership churn. This database allowed us to reach out to members who were showing up less, and helped us keep an eye out for potential e-board members and more. The database was structured like a relational database with automationations and filtering like SQL.

During the summer, one of the project directors left to create www.spreadjam.com so one of the spots on the project team was left vacant. I jumped at the opportunity and let other board members know that I would gladly step up and hold two positions. Despite my lack of experience in AI, they let me take on the role because it was easier than holding interviews to find another candidate. Over the summer, I spent the 5-9 after my internship learning about AI and creating a project.

I ended up teaching students how to use LLMs and Retrieval Augmented Generation to build structures in Minecraft. The project was a success and I was able to teach many students. One of my students, who was also inexperienced in AI, went on to learn a lot more and took over my position when I stepped down. I refined this the following semester to create a curriculum that taught beginners how recommendation algorithms worked and how to build a Letterboxd clone. authored 2 projects found at asdlfjasdfa.

Another rewarding part of being on the projects team was facilitating the advanced projects. The advanced project track at AI Club allowed members to work on full-stack projects that they created. I helped hold interviews for potential members and group leaders. Afterwards, I provided weekly assistance on architecture, model implementation, and deployment. Each semester this program was a success. Our project system acted as a funnel to Michigan State University's Burgess Institute where groups were able to pitch their ideas to receive funding. A number of projects went on to receive the funding and a majority of the projects are still being maintained and growing.

My time at the AI Club taught me a lot about leadership and how a leader works even when no one is watching. The events and projects we held were only the result of the countless hours we spent over the summers and breaks planning.
