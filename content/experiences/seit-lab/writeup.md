In 2024, I joined the SEIT Lab at Michigan State University to compete in the Mcity AV Challenge hosted by the Traffic Lab at the University of Michigan. The competition was to build an algorithm to allow a car to navigate a traffic environment, with sharp turns, traffic, traffic lights, and more. 

I worked as the primary algorithm developer, developing an algorithm to control the entire vehicle. At the time, I lacked experience with AI, including strategies like reinforcement learning and evolutionary computing, which, looking back on it, would have helped me greatly. However, that lack of knowledge allowed me to think more creatively and build a rule-based algorithm. I implemented algorithms I learned from my undergraduate algorithms class, like sliding windows, to replicate how I would drive. Despite not having a strong model that could solve the entire problem of self-driving vehicles, I attacked each problem individually.

The result of this unique approach was a second place finish out of 10 competing research labs. I was especially proud because other labs were made up of graduate students, while I was a rising junior undergraduate. This placing allowed my team to present at the competition's open house where I helped port the algorithm to a real vehicle. The presentation can be found at [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a0yztHbhxcw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a0yztHbhxcw).
