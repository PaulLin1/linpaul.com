I started to get obsessed over my to -do in the summer of 2026. Looking back at it, rightfully so. I was operating with a kitchen-sink monolith of various notes in Apple Notes and tasks, birthdays, and random stuff in Google Suite applications. This hectic approach led me to search for a way to consildate my to do and productivity applications.

Solution 1 was picking a side: either Apple or Google. As a user of both, both made sense. I had Apple devices that allowed me to sync information over iCloud, but I also had a Google account that did the same with Google Drive. Apple had robust task and calendar features; Google did too. Apple had Apple Notes, Google had ... Google Keep? I am not using that. Okay, so Apple it is.

After porting over my tasks and calendar to Apple, and keeping my notes in Apple Notes, my productivity streamlined. Everything worked well for a week, tasks and calenders were synced with clear widgets on my devices, until I realized I didn't solve any of my issues! My Apple Notes was still a monolith with notes that could've been in my to do list, notes that needed to be organized, and ones that didn't. I had no idea where each note was or if I even had a note on a given topic.

This led to the strenuous decision of utilizing folders and dedicated notes for each thing. Having a to-do list note that served as an extension to my reminders app. Having a goal note. Even having only one scrap note so I wasn't constantly making new ones. This seemed to solve all my problems. At least my text-based problems...

You see, at the same time, I was getting even more excited about Silk, a curation app. I loved screenshotting things and holding them in my photo album for 'future use,' but I never got around to doing whatever that was. I saw Silk as a unique tool to do that. However, I was still on the waitlist.

This led me to another genius solution: why don't I just vibe-code my own app? I could build it myself, but that would be quite time-consuming on top of finishing my degree. And even if I did work full time on the app, it would still be a while until it was ready for daily use. So, I vibe-coded for a week.

Each day, a new great new, personazlied feature was added, but I realized that it just wasn't cutting it. There was so much. I wanted image displays, a to-do list, tags, smart grouping, and Markdown editing. I couldn't have everything. Even working at the speed of Claude Code, it was not enough. But eventually, this sparked my best idea yet.

Two years ago, I ran into this same problem. I wanted to be an efficient student, taking notes and having a clear calendar and to-do list. I went through this entire charade. But what that led me to was Obsidian, a productivity app that seemingly had everything. I used it for two weeks, but it didn't stick. There was too many features, and I wanted to use them all. I bogged myself down with clutter till eventually the benefit of using one app was abstracting with many different microservices. But relearning about the tool now, and seeing how others have tried a minimalist approach with it, I was inspired again.

So I did the big port again, and it fit perfectly. It was not only a good productivity tool but a creative tool. It allowed me to rework the way I saw my thhoughts. I only used features and tools I needed. And with its plugin feature, I had the adaptibility of building my own app while only having to build unique features, not core ones.

The first plugin I made was Orbit, a smart tool list to balance my 10+ hobbies. Orbit has an algorithm similar to a queue, where the thing I did the furthest back is pushed to the top so I don't have long gaps. I didn't have to rebuild a database, Obsidian handled that with markdown files with properties. I didn't have to build a new UI framework, I could enherit Obsidian's ItemView. This conveniance gives me limitless possibilities of what my productuvity tool to be.

After a month of using Obsidian full time, I feel like I am finally content. Productivity is made easy, but I also have the excitement of new features and toys within Obsidian. If anything becomes too abstract, I can scale back within the same application. I realized that even if I wanted somplicitiy and coherency within my productivity suite, I could benefit from a more complex app by growing in places I want, and pruning where I dont.
