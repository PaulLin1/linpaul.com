After seeing the cover design for [Homer Radio](https://music.apple.com/us/curator/homer-radio/1620058858) featuring speakers in intricate patterns, I became interested in computational design again. I  briefly encountered the field while taking a class on evolutionary computation (CSE 848 at Michigan State University), which led me to make small projects of my own, like a boid simulations and neural architecture graphs. But seeing computational systems used as a serious design tool completely changed how I thought of it. It showed me that computation could be at the forefront of visual design rather than simply supporting it.

This led me to the work of James Maeda, particularly Design by Numbers. I was fascinated by how simple rules could produce work that felt organic and natural. Florada especially stood out to me. I decided to build my own system that generates flowers from a set of parameters. From there, I started combining the flower system with ideas from evolutionary computation. I experimented with boid-like simulations where flowers move through a space while rotating and responding to one another. This idea was also influenced by the work of [Danny Cole for Loukeman](https://www.instagram.com/p/DXh8y86jqTf/?img_index=1).

Eventually, I transformed the repository into a collection of modular tools for computational design, where individual systems can be combined to create different compositions and behaviors. Currently, the flower is the core primitive. Around it, I've built several different ways of composing and transforming these primitives, including a collage view and a rotational view. The goal is less about producing one specific artwork and more about building a flexible visual system where simple rules, primitives, and simulations can be recombined to produce increasingly complex results.
